Autonomous Underwater Vehicles (AUVs) operating in deep-ocean environments face severe hydrodynamic nonlinearities, water current disturbances, thruster delay, and GPS denial. Conventional analytical physics models simplify hydrodynamic drag and added mass, leading to accumulated dead-reckoning drift. Conversely, pure data-driven deep learning models are prone to overfitting high-frequency acoustic sensor noise and lack physical conservation guarantees. This work introduces a unified hybrid physics-AI dynamic modeling and goal-directed Model Predictive Control (MPC) framework for the Autosub6000 AUV using real deep-sea telemetry from the AURORA Mission M86 (31,944 samples at 0.50 Hz). A 3D marine kinematic prior with quadratic surge drag is coupled with a recurrent Gated Recurrent Unit (GRU) to learn unmodeled hydrodynamic residuals over a 10-step rolling history buffer. The validated hybrid model is embedded into an online receding-horizon MPC optimized via the Cross-Entropy Method (CEM) over a 20-second lookahead window (H = 10), rigorously respecting actuator slew limits and boundary constraints. Benchmarking demonstrates that the GRU residual model reduces one-step heading and surge velocity errors by 17.5% and 22.0% over the physics baseline. In closed-loop 3D trajectory tracking simulations, the hybrid LSTM-enhanced MPC achieves a position RMSE of 12.17 m, outperforming classical PID control (39.79 m RMSE) by 69.4% and physics-only MPC (116.79 m RMSE) by 89.6%, while rejecting environmental disturbances.

Keywords: Autonomous Underwater Vehicle (AUV), Physics-AI Hybrid Modeling, Gated Recurrent Unit (GRU), Model Predictive Control (MPC), Cross-Entropy Method, Deep-Sea Robotics.

CHAPTER 1: INTRODUCTION
1.1 Introduction (Background)

Autonomous Underwater Vehicles (AUVs) have emerged as irreplaceable instruments for deep-ocean scientific exploration, marine bathymetry mapping, transatlantic telecommunication infrastructure maintenance, and benthic ecology assessment. Unlike terrestrial autonomous ground vehicles or unmanned aerial systems that operate in air with continuous GPS lock, underwater robotic platforms function in an extreme physical regime characterized by complete electromagnetic signal attenuation, high hydrostatic pressures, and dense fluid dynamics. In seawater, radio frequency signals attenuate within millimeters, rendering satellite global navigation satellite systems (GNSS) entirely unusable once submerged.

Consequently, an AUV must navigate using dead-reckoning fusion combining Inertial Navigation Systems (INS), Doppler Velocity Logs (DVL), pressure depth sensors, and periodic acoustic transceivers such as Ultra-Short Baseline (USBL) arrays. However, water is approximately 800 times denser than air, introducing severe hydrodynamic forces—such as quadratic fluid drag, added mass, vortex shedding, and buoyancy-pitch coupling. Classical analytical marine models rely on idealized damping approximations and static coefficients. Over extended survey runs, unmodeled fluid disturbances, thruster propulsion lags, and ocean currents cause standard physics models to drift drastically, leading to poor trajectory tracking and potentially dangerous deviations from planned subsea inspection corridors.

1.2 Problem Statement

Deep-ocean trajectory tracking for long-endurance autonomous submarines is constrained by low-frequency telemetry sampling (0.50 Hz, sampling interval dt = 2.0 s), substantial unmodeled hydrodynamic damping, unmeasured ocean current drift, and non-Gaussian acoustic measurement noise. Classical linear controllers (such as Proportional-Integral-Derivative, PID) act purely reactively without preview capability, resulting in significant tracking phase lag during 3D curve maneuvers. Conversely, conventional Model Predictive Control (MPC) relying exclusively on simplified textbook physics fails due to substantial open-loop drift. Furthermore, purely data-driven black-box neural networks tend to overfit noisy acoustic USBL transponder updates and lack physical conservation guarantees. Thus, the fundamental problem is: How can we formulate an integrated control architecture that unifies first-principles marine physics with deep recurrent residual learning to achieve high-accuracy, disturbance-resilient 3D trajectory tracking while enforcing strict actuator slew rate and flight boundary constraints?

1.3 Objectives

To resolve this challenge, this project establishes four core engineering and scientific objectives:

  • 1. Formulate a Deterministic 3D Marine Physics Model: Establish a reduced-order baseline incorporating 3D kinematic translation and quadratic hydrodynamic surge drag calibrated to real ocean survey data.
  • 2. Synthesize a Deep Recurrent Residual Model: Train and benchmark Multi-Layer Perceptron (MLP), Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM), and Gated Recurrent Unit (GRU) architectures under strict chronological splitting to predict unmodeled dynamics residuals without corrupting physical position geometry.
  • 3. Design an Online Receding-Horizon MPC Controller: Formulate a multi-objective Model Predictive Controller utilizing the Cross-Entropy Method (CEM) over a 20-second lookahead horizon (H = 10 steps), enforcing physical rudder deflection, pitch rate, and speed rate limits.
  • 4. Comprehensive Validation & Benchmarking: Quantify single-step prediction RMSE across 7 state variables and evaluate closed-loop 3D trajectory tracking performance against baseline PID and physics-only MPC under current disturbances.
1.4 Contributions
  • A zero-data-leakage chronological benchmarking methodology on 31,944 samples of real Autosub6000 deep-ocean telemetry (AURORA Mission M86).
  • Discovery of the acoustic sensor noise paradox: proving that training neural networks directly on raw acoustic coordinates causes severe overfitting, whereas learning residuals on attitude, angular rates, and velocity yields massive predictive gains (+17.5% in heading, +22.0% in speed).
  • A high-performance vectorized CEM-MPC optimizer that achieves an unprecedented 12.17 m closed-loop position tracking RMSE, representing a 69.4% error reduction compared to tuned PID control and an 89.6% reduction over physics-only MPC.
CHAPTER 2: RELATED WORK
2.1 Comprehensive Survey

Marine craft dynamics and control have traditionally relied on the seminal formulations established by Fossen (2011), which model rigid-body kinematics coupled with 6-DOF nonlinear hydrodynamic damping, restoring forces, and added mass tensors. While mathematically rigorous, obtaining accurate hydrodynamic derivatives requires extensive tow-tank towing tests, planar motion mechanism experiments, or computationally intensive Reynolds-Averaged Navier-Stokes (RANS) CFD simulations. In real ocean deployments, marine growth, payload changes, and environmental turbulence continually alter these parameters, degrading model fidelity.

2.3 Challenges

1. Low Telemetry Frequency (0.50 Hz): Telemetry is logged every 2.0 seconds. High-frequency actuation dynamics must be inferred across long discrete integration steps.

2. Acoustic USBL Positioning Noise: Acoustic transponders suffer multipath echoes and sporadic positional jumps, creating non-Gaussian observation noise.

3. Multi-Step Closed-Loop Stability: Neural networks trained on single-step targets often diverge or compound errors when rolled out recursively over 10 future steps in predictive control.

4. Non-Convex Physical Constraints: Actuators have finite slew rates and pitch/rudder angle limits that standard linear controllers violate.

2.4 Motivation of the Project Work

The motivation is to establish an unassailable engineering framework where data and physics cooperate rather than compete. By enforcing deterministic kinematics for spatial translation, the vehicle is guaranteed geometric consistency. By assigning unmodeled fluid drag, pitch coupling, and thruster lag to a deep recurrent GRU/LSTM network, the controller gains foresight into real marine behaviors without suffering from acoustic sensor overfitting.

3.1 Mathematical Modelling / Datasets Description

The research utilizes telemetry from the Autosub6000 AUV during AURORA Mission M86 in the North Atlantic (IEEE DataPort). Autosub6000 is a 6.8 m long, 3.5-tonne exploration submarine equipped with a Teledyne RDI 300 kHz Doppler Velocity Log (DVL), IXSEA PHINS Inertial Navigation System, Digiquartz resonant pressure transducer, and USBL transponder.

3.2 Method(s) of Mathematical Modelling / Data Processing

To prevent severe data leakage inherent in random k-fold shuffling of time-series trajectories, the 31,944 samples were partitioned strictly chronologically: Train (70.0%, 22,360 samples), Validation (15.0%, 4,791 samples), and held-out Test (15.0%, 4,792 samples).

Continuous Feature Encoding: To avoid branch-cut angular discontinuities at [-pi, pi), heading is encoded continuously: s_t = [U_t, phi_t, theta_t, cos(psi_t), sin(psi_t), z_t]^T in R^6. A rolling temporal buffer of T = 10 timesteps (20.0 seconds of history) is fed to the neural network.

3.3 Programming Tools Details

The system is implemented in Python 3.10+ using PyTorch 2.x for GPU-accelerated recurrent residual modeling, NumPy/SciPy for vectorized matrix kinematics and CEM optimization, Pandas for temporal feature windowing, and Matplotlib/Seaborn for high-resolution 3D visualization.

CHAPTER 4: METHODOLOGY
4.1 Controller / Algorithm Design (Mathematical Equations with Description)

1. Analytical Marine Physics Baseline:

x_{t+1}^{phys} = x_t + U_t × cos(θ_t) × cos(ψ_t) × Δt
y_{t+1}^{phys} = y_t + U_t × cos(θ_t) × sin(ψ_t) × Δt
z_{t+1}^{phys} = z_t - U_t × sin(θ_t) × Δt
U_{t+1}^{phys} = max(U_t + K_d × (U_{cruise}^2 - U_t^2) × Δt, 0.0)

where K_d = 0.06073 is the hydrodynamic surge drag coefficient calibrated from sea trials.

2. Neural Residual Learning:

r_t = x_{t+1}^{actual} - x_{t+1}^{phys}
x_hat_{t+1} = x_{t+1}^{phys} + f_{θ}(S_{t-9:t})

where f_{θ} is a 2-layer Recurrent GRU (64 hidden units, 41,095 parameters) trained with Adam optimizer.

3. Model Predictive Control (MPC) Cost Function:

min_{u_{0:H-1}} J = ∑_{k=1}^H [ w_{pos} ||p_k - p_k^{ref}||^2 + w_{depth} (z_k - z_k^{ref})^2 + w_{head} (1 - cos(ψ_k - ψ_k^{ref})) + w_{Δu} ||Δu_k||^2 ] + J_{terminal}
4.2 Controller / Algorithm Design Steps

At each 2.0 s sampling instant, the MPC receives state x_t and reference path p_{1:H}^{ref}. The Cross-Entropy Method samples K = 70 candidate action sequences, clips them against physical actuator slew rates, and simulates forward rollouts using the hybrid model. The top 20% elite candidates iteratively update the sampling distribution over 3 cycles, and the optimal first action u_0* is commanded to the vehicle rudders and thrusters.

4.3 Project Architecture & Stability Analysis

Actuator boundaries enforce pitch rates ≤ 0.10 rad/step (2.86 deg/s), heading rates ≤ 0.20 rad/step (5.73 deg/s), and surge acceleration ≤ 0.15 m/s/step. Even under extreme disturbance, the deterministic kinematic formulation prevents unbounded runaway drift.

5.4 Discussion

1. The Acoustic Sensor Noise Paradox: On North and East coordinates, all neural networks worsened one-step predictions over physics. This proves that acoustic USBL transponder data contains high-frequency observation noise. Training networks directly on spatial coordinates results in overfitting to sensor jitter. Physics kinematics must handle spatial translation, while neural networks model velocity and orientation.

2. GRU vs. LSTM Multi-Step Horizons: Despite GRU having slightly lower single-step RMSE, LSTM achieved a vastly superior closed-loop tracking RMSE of 12.17 m vs. 81.98 m for GRU. The LSTM's decoupled cell state memory acts as a stabilizing integrator that prevents recursive error accumulation across multi-step horizons.

CHAPTER 6: CONCLUSION AND FUTURE DIRECTIONS
6.1 Conclusion

This laboratory project designed, implemented, and validated a unified hybrid physics-AI dynamic modeling and Model Predictive Control framework for an autonomous underwater vehicle. Benchmarked against 31,944 samples from the Autosub6000 AURORA mission, our approach proves that combining deterministic marine physics with recurrent residual learning eliminates the drift of classical models and prevents acoustic overfitting. The LSTM-Enhanced MPC achieved a position tracking RMSE of 12.17 m, delivering a 69.4% improvement over PID control and 89.6% over physics-only MPC.

6.2 Future Directions
  • Extended Kalman Filter Fusion: Clean acoustic USBL transceiver noise via an EKF before feeding features to the neural network.
  • Online Recursive Least Squares: Adaptively calibrate hydrodynamic drag and added mass parameters during real-time navigation.
  • Hardware-in-the-Loop Implementation: Deploy the vectorized PyTorch CEM-MPC pipeline onto an onboard NVIDIA Jetson Orin embedded computer.
